    How reminders behave

    Every reminder is its own card
    Answering a card affects only that reminder: Done on Move doesn't clear Water. All cards are the same width; their height fits what's on them.
    They stack, one on top
    If more come due while a card is showing, they wait behind it, peeking out above ("Next: Posture", "+1" if there are more). Answer the front card and the next one comes forward. Each reminder is in the stack at most once; if several come due together, bigger cards go first.
    Timers count only while you're working
    With the When to remind settings on, timers pause while you're away and outside active hours, and reminders wait until fullscreen apps, calls or presentations end. Slim cards that waited more than 10 minutes are skipped.
    Previews don't count
    Preview shows a reminder exactly as it will look, without restarting its timer.

      When to remind

      Reminders only come when they make sense: during your working hours, while you're at the desk, and not in the middle of a presentation. Turn off any of these you don't want.

      Pause when away
      Timers stop while you're idle or the screen is locked. If you locked the screen or your PC went to sleep, the move timer restarts when you're back, since you got up.

      How to sit well

      Set it up once, check it a few times a day, and get up often. Even the best position gets stiff if you hold it for hours, so the best posture is the next one.

      1. Ears over shoulders. Chin slightly tucked. If your head drifts forward, it gets much heavier for your neck.
      2. Screen top at eye level, about an arm's length away, straight in front of you. On a laptop, raise it and use a separate keyboard.
      3. Shoulders relaxed, down and slightly back, not shrugged toward your ears.
      4. Elbows about 90°, close to your sides, wrists straight. Keyboard and mouse at elbow height.
      5. Lower back supported. Keep the gentle inward curve. Use the chair's lumbar support or a small cushion.
      6. Hips all the way back in the seat, with weight even on both sit bones.
      7. Knees about 90°, level with or a bit below your hips, with a fist-width gap between the seat edge and the back of your knee.
      8. Feet flat on the floor (or a footrest), legs uncrossed.

      The 10-second reset

      1Scoot your hips all the way back
      2Roll shoulders up, back, and let them drop
      3Tuck your chin slightly, as if making a double chin
      4Breathe out and relax. No need to stiffen up

      Common drifts and their fixes

      Twisted or turned to one side
      Usually the screen or keyboard is off-center. Put the main monitor and keyboard directly in front of your belly button. If you use two screens, center the one you use most.
      Sliding forward and slumping
      Your hips have crept to the front of the seat. Scoot back so your lower back touches the backrest. If your feet don't reach the floor, add a footrest instead of perching.
      Head poking toward the screen
      Often the text is too small or the screen too far or too low. Raise the screen, bring it closer, or zoom in.
      Leaning on one elbow / crossed legs
      Fine for a minute, but it twists the pelvis if you stay there. Uncross, plant both feet, and center your weight.
      Shrugged shoulders
      The desk or armrests are too high. Lower them, or raise your chair and add a footrest.
